## 1. HKD tax reform: legal basis, thresholds, e-invoices, bookkeeping, declaration periods, how many HKD are affected, and what happened in 2026

### Takeaway
Lump-sum tax ended on 01/01/2026, and every HKD now declares its own tax. The legal chain runs from Resolution 68-NQ/TW to the Law on Tax Administration 108/2025/QH15 and then Decree 68/2026/NĐ-CP. Decree 141/2026/NĐ-CP, signed 29/04/2026 and applied back to 01/01/2026, raised the VAT/PIT exemption threshold to **1 billion VND per year**. As a result, about **2.56 million HKD pay no VAT or PIT**, but they must still keep a sales book (S1a-HKD), notify the tax authority of their bank and e-wallet accounts, and file their **first-ever annual revenue notification by 31/01/2027**. The heavy obligations now fall on a much smaller group above 1 billion VND (about 270,000 HKD by a 2025 estimate): cash-register e-invoices, quarterly declarations, a choice of PIT method, and input invoices. In practice 2026 brought confusion, rule changes in the middle of the year, stall closures in HCMC markets, and a VCCI survey in which e-invoicing and fixing errors were the top pains.

#### Declaration periods, rates and obligations
- Deadlines:
  - Quarterly filers: the last day of the first month of the next quarter.
  - Monthly filers: the 20th of the following month.
  - HKD under the threshold: an annual revenue notification by 31/01 of the next year.
  - If revenue crosses the threshold during the year, declarations start from the quarter in which it crossed.
  - — [Thư viện Pháp luật](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/phap-luat-doanh-nghiep/bai-viet/cach-xac-dinh-ho-kinh-doanh-khai-thue-theo-thang-hay-theo-quy-tu-05-3-2026-19345.html) (search-summary)
- Q1/2026 arrangements:
  - Monthly filers' deadlines for January–March were extended to 30/04/2026.
  - The tax system pre-filled suggested declarations.
  - The Ministry of Finance issued a handbook.
  - A 15-day guidance campaign ("D15") started on 10/03/2026.
  - — [xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn, 12/03/2026](https://xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn/luu-y-chinh-trong-ky-khai-thue-quy-i-2026-119260312174235595.htm)
  - Because of the holidays, the Q1 deadline effectively became 04/05/2026. — [VOV](https://vov.vn/kinh-te/ho-kinh-doanh-ke-khai-nop-thue-quy-i2026-3-viec-can-lam-ngay-post1281366.vov) (search-summary)
- Under the 2026 percentage method, PIT is charged only on revenue **above** the threshold, while VAT is charged on total revenue. Example for rental: VAT = revenue × 5%; PIT = (revenue − threshold) × 5%. — [xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn](https://xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn/luu-y-chinh-trong-ky-khai-thue-quy-i-2026-119260312174235595.htm)

- **Bank accounts:**
  - HKD must notify the tax authority of all bank accounts and e-wallets used to receive business money, using Form 01/BK-STK.
  - Existing HKD had to do this by **20/04/2026**.
  - The legal basis is Decree 68/2026, Circular 18/2026 and Law 108/2025.
  - — [Báo Chính phủ, 07/03/2026](https://baochinhphu.vn/tu-2026-ho-kinh-doanh-phai-bao-co-quan-thue-cac-tai-khoan-nhan-tien-102260307185152112.htm); [LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/truoc-20-4-2026-ho-kinh-doanh-phai-thong-bao-tai-khoan-ngan-hang-vi-dien-tu-cho-co-quan-thue-186-107554-article.html) (search-summary)

### Inferences
- **The reform split HKD into two segments:**
  - **Segment 1: about 2.56M "exempt but must still comply a little" HKD.** They need an S1a-HKD sales book, a bank-account notification, and the 01/TKN-CNKD notification by 31/01/2027. What they need is reassurance, effortless record-keeping, and proof that revenue stayed at or below 1B. They are likely to pay close to nothing.
  - **Segment 2: about 270,000+ HKD above 1B** (2025 cash-register estimate). They face heavy duties: e-invoices, quarterly filings, a choice of PIT method, input invoices, and expense evidence for the income method. They already pay for POS and e-invoicing, so there is willingness to pay.
- **The rules themselves keep changing, and that is a pain point.** The threshold moved from 100M to 500M to 1B within about four months of 2026, forcing HKD to re-file, and lobbying for 2–2.5B continues. A product therefore needs:
  - updatable rule configuration;
  - a memory of each HKD's profile, so it can tell each owner "what changed for you".
- **The deadlines line up with the competition.**
  - Round 2 (23/11/2026–10/01/2027) falls during preparation for the first-ever annual revenue notification and the Q4/2026 quarterly filing. Under the rules above, both are due 31/01/2027.
  - The final (27/02/2027) comes just after, so an MVP can be piloted against a real deadline.
- **The tax authority's own automation shrinks the value of pure form-filling.** Under Law 108 Art. 13, declarations are generated automatically from connected e-invoice and POS data. The value moves to three places:
  - HKD without POS, whose revenue arrives as cash plus transfers to several accounts;
  - reconciliation, exception handling and risk alerts;
  - collecting evidence for input invoices and expenses.

## 3. E-commerce sellers: platform tax withholding (Decree 117/2025), other compliance pains, and seller counts

### Takeaway
Since 01/07/2025, platforms with a payment function withhold VAT and PIT on every transaction, whatever the seller's annual revenue (1.5% for goods). Sellers whose total annual revenue is below the exemption threshold must claim refunds themselves on Form 03/CNKD-TMĐT, and **a refund fails if the seller's ID or tax code is wrong or missing**. The threshold rose to 1B VND for 2026 and is counted once per person across all channels. As a result, a large share of the roughly 620,000 shops with orders are probably refund-eligible for 2026. To check, each seller must add up off-platform revenue (Facebook, Zalo, bank transfers) as well. Separately, from 01/07/2026 the E-commerce Law adds identity-verification and livestream-content duties.

### Cited Findings
- **Decree 117/2025/NĐ-CP** was issued 09/06/2025 and took effect 01/07/2025. Domestic and foreign platforms with payment functions withhold VAT and PIT on each transaction. Sellers must provide an accurate tax ID or personal ID. Sellers below the threshold can claim refunds. — [LSVN](https://lsvn.vn/nhung-noi-dung-moi-tai-nghi-dinh-so-117-2025-nd-cp-ve-quan-ly-thue-doi-voi-kinh-doanh-tren-nen-tang-thuong-mai-dien-tu-a159464.html)
  - Withholding happens as soon as the system confirms a successful transaction. Sellers whose tax was withheld do not declare those platform sales again. — [Báo Đầu tư](https://baodautu.vn/tu-17-san-thuong-mai-dien-tu-nop-thue-ho-cho-nha-ban-hang-online-d306251.html) (search-summary)
- **Withholding rates:** goods 1% VAT + 0.5% PIT (1.5% in total); services 5% + 2% (7%); transport and services attached to goods 3% + 1.5%. — [Kế toán Thuận Thiên](https://ketoanthuanthien.vn/kien-thuc/nop-thay-thue-cua-san-tmdt/)
  - Withholding applies to "tất cả các cá nhân có hoạt động kinh doanh trên các nền tảng TMĐT", with no revenue threshold.
  - Sellers on platforms without a payment function declare and pay themselves.
  - Refunds are claimed on Form 03/CNKD-TMĐT and take **6–40 working days**. (The article uses the 2025 threshold and does not cover the 2026 decrees.)
- **Refund rules:** — [ACMAN](https://acman.vn/hoan-thue-ca-nhan-ho-kinh-doanh-duoc-san-tmdt-khai-thay-theo-nghi-dinh-117-2025-nd-cp.html)
  - Platforms withhold regardless of annual revenue.
  - A refund is due when the seller's total annual revenue falls in the non-taxable range. The seller submits Form 03/CNKD-TMĐT to the tax office for their place of residence.
  - "Trường hợp không cập nhật hoặc sai mã định danh/mã số thuế, cơ quan thuế không thể xử lý hoàn thuế" (from the Hanoi Tax e-commerce division's guidance, updated 15/11/2025).
- **Later law confirms the regime:**
  - Decree 68/2026: platforms with a payment function must "khấu trừ, kê khai và nộp thuế thay cho người bán" on each transaction, and the threshold is counted once per person across all locations. — [xcyber](https://xcyber.vn/nghi-dinh-68-2026-nd-cp/)
  - Law 108/2025 Art. 13(4) puts withholding on platforms. — [Krông Bông portal](https://krongbong.daklak.gov.vn/diem-moi-cua-luat-quan-ly-thue-so-108-2025-qh15-ap-dung-doi-voi-ho-kinh-doanh-ca-nhan-kinh-doanh-187.html)
  - Law 108/2025 also adds new provisions on tax declaration for e-commerce. — [MISA SME](https://sme.misa.vn/347631/luat-quan-ly-thue/) (search-summary)
- **Market (Metric.vn, reported by CafeF on 02/02/2026):** — [CafeF](https://cafef.vn/shopee-va-tiktok-shop-om-97-thi-phan-cuoc-choi-song-ma-va-su-thanh-loc-nha-ban-hang-188260202083449479.chn)
  - 2025 revenue across 4 platforms: **429.7 trillion VND**, up about 35%.
  - Shares: Shopee 56.04%, TikTok Shop 41.31% (97% combined). Shopee was down from 64% at the end of 2024.
  - **Shops with orders fell 7.43%, or about 50,000 shops**, while revenue grew 34.75%.
  - Mall shops are 2.12% of sellers but earn 32.6% of revenue.
  - Causes cited: the new E-commerce Law and the tightened identity and tax rules (Decree 117/2025).
  - Search-summary only (page not opened): more than 86,300 Shopee sellers had no orders in 2024, and more than 55,000 TikTok Shop sellers left. — [VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/shopee-hut-hoi-tiktok-shop-lan-san-thi-phan.htm); [Metric insights](https://metric.vn/insights/category/metric/ecom-market-research/)
- **E-commerce Law 2025 (effective 01/07/2026):** — [Công dân & Khuyến học, 26/05/2026](https://congdankhuyenhoc.vn/tu-1-7-2026-livestream-ban-hang-se-khong-con-vung-xam-kol-koc-co-the-bi-ngat-song-ngay-lap-tuc-179260526154000695.htm)
  - Platforms must collect, verify and keep the identity of livestream sellers.
  - They must **immediately stop** any livestream that sells prohibited goods, shows illegal content, or advertises unverified products.
  - They must keep livestream images, audio and transaction content for **at least 12 months**.
  - Sellers must provide documents on origin, quality and business conditions before going live. Regulated items (functional foods, cosmetics, drugs) need the required certificates.

### Inferences
- **Seller count, derived from the Metric figures:** if about 50,000 shops equal 7.43% of the 2024 total, then about **673,000 shops had orders in 2024 and about 623,000 in 2025**. This excludes sellers who sell only through social media.
- **Revenue concentration:**
  - Non-Mall shops (about 97.9% of sellers) earned about 67.4% of 429.7T VND, roughly 290T VND, or an average of about **475M VND per non-Mall shop**.
  - Revenue is heavily skewed, so the median is much lower. Most individual sellers are therefore probably below 1B and refund-eligible for the 1.5% withheld on goods in 2026, unless they have large off-platform revenue.
  - *Illustrative assumption, not sourced:* if sub-1B sellers account for 25–35% of platform revenue, about 1.6–2.3 trillion VND/year would be withheld and refundable.
- **The pain is a chain of steps:**
  1. Collect statements from several platforms and add off-platform transfers.
  2. Work out the annual total, counted once per person.
  3. Decide whether a refund is due, or whether 1B was crossed (which triggers e-invoices within 30 days and quarterly filings).
  4. File 03/CNKD-TMĐT and the annual 01/TKN-CNKD.
  5. Fix any ID or tax-code mismatches.
  6. Track the refund.

  This is a typical multi-step agentic workflow, and it ends in a concrete amount of money.
- The livestream duties in the E-commerce Law create a second compliance pain for sellers and KOCs (see §4).

### Gaps
- Whether Decree 68/2026 or 141/2026 stopped withholding for sellers under 1B: no evidence either way. Every source found still describes withholding "regardless of revenue".
- The number of refund claims filed or paid in 2025–2026, and the amounts: not found.
- Third-party API access to settlement and withholding data (Shopee Open Platform, TikTok Shop Partner API, Lazada Open Platform): not verified.
- The official number of the E-commerce Law and its implementing decree: not verified.
- The number of sellers who sell only on Facebook or Zalo: not found.
- Whether a third party may file refund requests for sellers without being a licensed tax agent: not verified.

## 4. Advertising compliance: the amended Law on Advertising, KOL rules after the 2025 scandals, and demand for automated claim checking and livestream monitoring

### Takeaway
Vietnam has moved from indirect to direct liability for KOLs:
- **Law No. 75/2025/QH15** (effective 01/01/2026) requires influencers to verify advertisers and products, disclose advertising before and during content, and avoid partners that the state has found in violation.
- **Decree 87/2026/NĐ-CP** (effective 15/05/2026) fines individuals **40–100M VND per violation**.
- **The E-commerce Law** (effective 01/07/2026) makes platforms cut off violating livestreams and keep recordings for 12 months.
- **The Kera candy verdict** (19/11/2025: prison for three KOLs) made the risk real.

Local tools found so far verify KOL identity or credentials (KOL Plus, VCCA Media Pass). This research found no local tool that checks claims automatically against a product's legal file, but the search was cut short.

### Inferences
- **Why there is demand:**
  - Individual fines of 40–100M VND per violation, a real prison precedent, and platforms' power to cut off a livestream mean a single risky sentence can cost a KOC more than several months of income.
  - This creates a strong reason to pre-check scripts and to have live alerts during a stream.
  - Multi-channel networks (MCNs), agencies and brands share the reputational and contractual risk.
- **The job is genuinely agentic.** It involves:
  1. gathering the product's legal file (công bố sản phẩm, xác nhận nội dung quảng cáo);
  2. extracting the claims the product is allowed to make;
  3. checking scripts, videos and live speech against those claims and against a list of prohibited expressions;
  4. inserting ad disclosures;
  5. keeping evidence for 12 months;
  6. escalating doubtful cases to a person.
- **Who pays:** brands, MCNs, agencies and e-commerce sellers that run livestreams (B2B). Long-tail KOCs work as a freemium funnel.

## 6. Open banking (Circular 64/2024/TT-NHNN) and bank-transaction webhooks: what data a startup can realistically access in 2026

### Takeaway
Circular 64/2024 (effective 01/03/2025; full compliance by **01/03/2027**) standardises banks' Open APIs:
- **Customer-data APIs** (account list, account details, transaction history) can serve third parties with the customer's consent, under the bank's terms.
- **Payment-initiation APIs** are limited to banks and licensed payment intermediaries.

In practice, a student startup in 2026 should get transaction data through aggregators and take payments through payOS/VietQR:
- **SePay:** direct APIs with 11 banks, SMS-banking parsing for the rest, webhooks, and a free tier.
- **Casso:** official APIs plus RPA, read-only.

**Agentic workflow**
1. **Dossier Agent:** reads the product's documents (công bố sản phẩm, xác nhận nội dung quảng cáo, label, test reports). It extracts an allow-list of claims, plus the required warnings, and keeps them in memory for each product.

4. **Evidence and Report Agent:** keeps time-stamped transcripts and recordings for 12 months or more, in line with the E-commerce Law's retention rule. It produces a compliance report for the brand or MCN and escalates doubtful cases to a human legal reviewer.
5. **Partner-Risk Agent:** checks brands and partners against public violation notices, since Law 75 forbids partnering with violators. The data source still needs to be verified.
6. **Regulation Watcher:** monitors new decrees and sanctions.

Tools: PhoWhisper ASR, OCR, a vision-language model, retrieval (RAG) over Law 75/2025, Decree 87/2026, the E-commerce Law and product-category rules, OBS/RTMP ingest, Zalo or Telegram alerts, storage.

**MVP scope (7 weeks)**
1. A pre-check web app for scripts and videos in two categories (cosmetics and health supplements).
2. A live monitor for the user's own stream, with alerts within about 5 seconds.
3. Report export.
4. About 100 curated rules, reviewed by a law lecturer.

Evaluate precision and recall on 50–100 real videos.

**Pilot access near HCMC**
- TDTU student creators and KOCs (assumption).
- Small HCMC agencies and MCNs, and local cosmetics and food SMEs.
- A live booth demo: a visitor says a claim and watches it get flagged.

**Revenue model and price benchmarks**
- B2B SaaS at 2–10M VND/month per agency or brand (assumption).
- Per-video checks for KOCs at 20–50k VND (assumption).
- Live-monitoring hours, and an audit-report add-on.
- No verified local price benchmarks.

**Key risks**
- Missed violations create liability, so the product must be advisory with human review.
- The legal knowledge base needs constant upkeep.
- Official product files are hard to access.
- B2B sales cycles are slow.
- Platform terms may restrict capturing streams.
- Scanning other people's content raises sensitivity issues.

#### Idea D: "Hoàn Thuế Sàn", an agentic tax-back and threshold-guard agent for online sellers on several channels (new idea)

**Target users and payer**
- **Users:** individual and household sellers on Shopee, TikTok Shop and Lazada who also sell through Facebook or Zalo; student sellers and KOCs.
- **Payers:** the sellers themselves, through a success fee or an annual plan. Later, accounting firms or partner programmes.

**Pain evidence**
- Platforms withhold 1.5% on goods (7% on services) whatever the seller's revenue.
- The seller must file the refund (03/CNKD-TMĐT) personally, and an ID mismatch blocks it.
- The threshold is counted once per person, so revenue must be added up across all channels.
- About 50,000 shops dropped out in 2025, which Metric partly blames on tax and ID rules.
- Sources: §3.

**Why now**
- Decree 117/2025 (effective 01/07/2025).
- Decree 68/2026 (05/03/2026).
- Decree 141/2026 (29/04/2026), applying back to 01/01/2026, so far more 2026 sellers fall under 1B and are refund-eligible (inference).
- **The first annual revenue notification is due 31/01/2027.**
- E-commerce Law (01/07/2026).

**Market size**
- **TAM:** about 623,000 shops with orders (inferred), plus social-media-only sellers (unknown).
- **Refundable pool:** about 1.6–2.3T VND/year (illustrative assumption, §3).
- **SOM:** 2,000–5,000 sellers × an average refund of 3–9M VND × a 10–15% fee ≈ 0.6–6.8B VND/year (assumption).

**Competitors and gap**
- No dedicated refund or threshold agent was found, but the search was incomplete.
- Existing help comes from accounting firms and tax agents, and from the multi-channel sales tools of MISA eShop and Sapo Omni, which are not refund-focused.
- **Gap:** a cross-channel, refund-first product that also fixes ID mismatches and guards the threshold.

**Agentic workflow**
1. **Statement Collector:** guides the seller to export settlement and withholding statements from each platform (APIs later), parses PDF/CSV, and brings in off-platform inflows through SePay webhooks.
2. **Revenue Aggregator:** removes duplicates between platform payouts and bank inflows, adds up the person's annual revenue across channels, and sorts goods from services (different rates).
3. **Eligibility and Threshold Agent:** decides whether a refund is due (at or below 1B) and how much. It warns before 1B is crossed, since crossing triggers e-invoice registration within 30 days and quarterly filing.
4. **Identity Checker:** compares the ID and tax code registered with each platform against the tax record (from a screenshot the user provides), flags mismatches that would block a refund, and drafts update requests to the platforms.
5. **Filing Agent:** pre-fills 03/CNKD-TMĐT and 01/TKN-CNKD. **The user signs and submits through eTax** (human in the loop). It also keeps a deadline calendar.
6. **Tracker Agent:** follows the refund status (from notices the user forwards) and follows up.

**MVP scope (7 weeks)**
- Parsers for Shopee and TikTok Shop statements; the aggregator; the eligibility calculator; form pre-fill; a Zalo or Telegram bot.
- Pilot with 20–50 student sellers between December and January.
- Demo result to aim for: "found X million VND refundable across 30 sellers".

**Pilot access near HCMC**
- TDTU student sellers and KOCs, and HCMC seller communities (assumption).

**Revenue model**
- A success fee of 10–15% of refunds (assumption).
- A "tax health" plan at 199k–299k VND/year (assumption).
- An upsell to bookkeeping (Idea A) for sellers who cross 1B.

**Key risks**
- A rule change that stops withholding for sellers under the threshold would kill the refund use case.
- Refunds take 6–40 working days, so real money may arrive after the final.
- Filing on someone's behalf may require tax-agent status (unverified); keeping the user as the filer avoids this.
- Platform terms on data.
- Small tickets for tiny sellers.

#### Idea E: "KếToán Agent Hub", a B2B2C agentic back office for accounting and tax-service providers that serve HKD (new idea)

## 8. Ranked shortlist and saturated ideas to avoid in this domain

### Takeaway
**Recommended ranking for finance and SME services:**
1. **B, ClaimGuard Live (33/40).**
2. **D, Hoàn Thuế Sàn (31/40).**
3. **A, Sổ Chợ AI (27/40)**, best pitched together with D as one platform: an agentic tax companion for micro-sellers, offline and online.
4. **E, KếToán Agent Hub (26/40).**
5. **C, Trọ Agent (24/40).**

Avoid "yet another" POS or sales app, e-invoice issuer, VietQR payment-notification tool, generic tax Q&A chatbot, HKD accounting app, landlord billing app, or KOL identity badge. Each is already served, often for free, by well-funded incumbents, banks or the state.

### Inferences
- **Why B ranks first in this domain:**
  - It has the strongest mix of why-now (three 2026 legal instruments, with fines and kill-switches), agentic depth (multimodal, real-time, evidence-keeping) and poster-booth appeal (scandal awareness and a live demo).
  - Its weakness is unproven willingness to pay and liability. Mitigate this by positioning it as an advisory co-pilot with human review, and by starting with one vertical (health supplements and cosmetics), where claims are most regulated.
- **Why D ranks second:**
  - It is a concrete "money back" outcome that students immediately understand.
  - Its timing is perfect: the first annual cycle closes 31/01/2027, just before the final on 27/02/2027.
  - Its weakness is dependence on today's withholding and refund rules. Pitch it as the entry point into broader tax compliance for micro-sellers (Idea A), so judges see a company rather than a feature.
- **Merged A + D ("agentic tax companion for micro-sellers, offline and online"):**
  - Merging lifts A's novelty (the refund wedge, aggregation across channels) and its student appeal, while keeping A's policy and social-impact story (Resolution 68, elderly market traders).
  - Estimated merged scores (researcher judgement): agentic 5, pain/why-now 4, novelty 3–4, market 3, MVP 3–4 (wider scope), risk 3, students 4–5, judges 4–5.
  - Within the finance domain, this is the most "fundable-sounding" narrative; B is the more distinctive demo.
- **Ideas likely to draw regulatory scepticism from judges** (not researched in this session; judgement only): AI stock-picking or robo-advice, peer-to-peer lending or alternative credit scoring, and crypto tools. In Vietnam these generally need licences, so avoid them for a student MVP.
- **Generic personal-finance trackers and expense apps:** likely saturated, but not verified in this session.
