## Q1. Electronic medical records (EMR / bệnh án điện tử): legal basis, deadlines, 2026 status, documentation burden, patient volume, number of facilities

### Takeaway
Circular 13/2025/TT-BYT (effective 21/07/2025) set 30/09/2025 as the EMR deadline for hospitals and 31/12/2026 for all other facilities. Hospitals missed their deadline at scale, so the Minister of Health issued an acceleration directive on 09/04/2026, and paper records are to end in all hospitals from 01/01/2027. That date falls inside the competition's Round 2. HCMC hospitals are almost fully on EMR (163/164), but the burden has moved onto doctors. They see 65–100 patients a day (about 7 minutes each), often keep paper and EMR in parallel, and incomplete notes now trigger automatic BHYT (social health insurance) payment rejections.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis & deadlines**
- The MoH issued Circular 13/2025/TT-BYT guiding EMR deployment on 06/06/2025. It took effect 21/07/2025 and repealed Circular 46/2018/TT-BYT (EMR) and parts of Circular 54/2017/TT-BYT. Sources: [ThuVienPhapLuat](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/The-thao-Y-te/Thong-tu-13-2025-TT-BYT-huong-dan-trien-khai-ho-so-benh-an-dien-tu-660113.aspx) (search summary for the issue date and repeals); [CaseLaw.vn](https://caselaw.vn/bai-viet/thong-tu-13-2025-tt-byt-huong-dan-trien-khai-ho-so-benh-an-dien-tu-tu-ngay-21-07-2025)

Converting old paper records follows Decree 137/2024/NĐ-CP on e-transactions. Sources: [Xây dựng chính sách (Government portal)](https://xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn/lo-trinh-trien-khai-ho-so-benh-an-dien-tu-tai-cac-benh-vien-119250610163849953.htm); [BHXH Việt Nam](https://baohiemxahoi.gov.vn/tintuc/Pages/linh-vuc-bao-hiem-y-te.aspx?ItemID=24947&CateID=169)

**Documentation burden & patient volume**
- BHYT pays 100% only for up to 65 visits per doctor per 8 hours. Visits from the 66th are paid at 50%, and nothing is paid if this persists for more than 3 months. That leaves "slightly over 7 minutes" per patient for history, exam, orders, diagnosis, prescription, data entry and explanation. A dermatologist can see 80–100 cases a day; the National Children's Hospital handles 4,000–5,000 visits a day. Source: [VietNamNet, 11/05/2026](https://vietnamnet.vn/bhyt-khong-thanh-toan-hon-900-trieu-benh-vien-ly-giai-viec-bac-si-phai-boi-hoan-2514257.html)

  - Jan–Aug 2026: 130.7 million visits (+2.1% y/y) and about 118,000 billion VND spent (+11%). Source: [Sức khỏe & Đời sống](https://suckhoedoisong.vn/hon-130-trieu-luot-kham-chua-benh-bhyt-trong-8-thang-tiep-tuc-de-xuat-tang-quyen-loi-cho-nguoi-tham-gia-bhyt-16926092220501841.htm) (search summary)

  - Average outpatient visit: 589,965 VND.

  Source: [Người Lao Động, 09/12/2025](https://tuoitre.vn/nld/nam-2025-chi-kham-chua-benh-bhyt-tai-tphcm-co-the-vuot-du-toan-khoang-2000-ti-dong-196251209193118437.htm)
- Global evidence on AI scribes (no Vietnamese data in the source): a JAMA study (April 2026) found clinicians saved about 16 minutes a day on documentation (about 10%), and primary-care doctors about 27 minutes. About 40% of Australian GPs use AI scribes (RACGP, November 2025). Source: [VJST, 26/08/2026](https://vjst.vn/nhung-cong-cu-ai-ho-tro-bac-si-co-thuc-su-giup-tiet-kiem-thoi-gian-102829.html)

### Inferences
- The EMR mandate makes complete, structured notes a compliance and money issue (BHYT auto-rejections, doctors repaying), not just a convenience. This is the strongest "necessity" argument for any documentation agent.
- Hospitals are mostly digitised. The remaining frontier is the roughly 10,000 private specialty clinics and polyclinics in HCMC that face the 31/12/2026 deadline, often with little IT staff. This is an inference from the HCMC DoH barrier list; no clinic-level adoption data were found.
- Competition timing: Round 2 (23/11/2026–10/01/2027) straddles the 31/12/2026 EMR deadline and the 01/01/2027 paper ban, which gives a ready-made "why now" slide.

### Gaps
- No official nationwide count of private clinics or of their EMR adoption. The 1,650-hospital denominator is unconfirmed.
- No Vietnamese time-motion study measuring how many minutes doctors spend on documentation.
- The source does not name the regulation behind the 65-visit cap.
- A lead that Military Central Hospital 108 runs AI voice-to-text into EMR with more than 85% accuracy appeared only in a search summary with no identifiable source page. It is unverified and not used above.

## Q2. Chronic disease (hypertension, diabetes), ageing, post-discharge follow-up, family-doctor model, telehealth

### Takeaway
Vietnam has about 16.9 million adults with hypertension but only about 15% have it under control. About 7 million people have diabetes, and more than 60% of them do not know it. The population is ageing faster than anywhere else in Asia. Resolution 72-NQ/TW makes free annual check-ups and lifelong e-health records national policy from 2026; HCMC aims to screen all of its roughly 15 million residents by 31/12/2026. A draft circular would let BHYT pay for family-medicine, home and remote care from 01/01/2027, including package payments that cover IT costs. The bottleneck is follow-up: some commune stations have one doctor for more than 7,000 residents.

### Cited Findings
- WHO Vietnam Hypertension Profile 2025: about 16.9 million adults aged 30–79 have hypertension; 51% are diagnosed, 32% treated and about 15% controlled. Presented at the 20th National Cardiology Congress (03/10/2026). Sources: [VnExpress, 04/10/2026](https://vnexpress.net/gan-17-trieu-nguoi-viet-mac-tang-huyet-ap-5128148.html); [Dân trí, 03/10/2026](https://dantri.com.vn/suc-khoe/viet-nam-dat-muc-tieu-kiem-soat-huyet-ap-cho-50-nguoi-benh-20261003181000418.htm)
- About half of people with hypertension and more than 60% of people with diabetes do not know they have the disease. The Vietnam Heart Association's "Charter 50/30" targets at least 50% blood-pressure control by 2030. Source: [Dân trí](https://dantri.com.vn/suc-khoe/viet-nam-dat-muc-tieu-kiem-soat-huyet-ap-cho-50-nguoi-benh-20261003181000418.htm)
- "Relying only on hospitals to manage patients will struggle to meet demand"; primary care must strengthen early detection and long-term follow-up. Source: [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/gan-17-trieu-nguoi-viet-mac-tang-huyet-ap-5128148.html)

### Inferences
- Resolution 72 creates a huge detection wave (millions of newly found hypertension and diabetes cases) without matching follow-up capacity. An agentic care coordinator can fill that gap.
- If adopted on 01/01/2027, the draft package payment is the first clear BHYT money flow that explicitly includes IT costs for health management. It could pay for follow-up agents delivered through family-medicine providers.
- 90-day chronic prescriptions leave long gaps between visits, which are a natural slot for automated check-ins and refill coordination.

### Gaps
- No Vietnamese data found on 30-day readmissions or post-discharge follow-up rates.
- No 2025–2026 telehealth adoption statistics (users or visits).
- No count of licensed family-medicine clinics in HCMC, and no published detection rates from HCMC's 2026 screening campaign.

## Q3. Pharmacies: number of outlets, e-prescription and national system connection, antibiotics without prescription, 2025 counterfeit scandals and the regulatory response

### Takeaway
Vietnam has about 60,000 retail drug outlets, 15,611 of them in HCMC, and they now operate under hard rules:
- E-prescriptions are mandatory (Circular 26/2025).
- Sales data must sync to the national drug database, backfilled to 01/01/2026 (Official Dispatch 934, 12/08/2026).
- Since 15/05/2026, selling prescription drugs (including antibiotics) without a prescription costs 10–20 million VND for an individual (Decree 90/2026).

The 2025 counterfeit milk, drug and functional-food scandals also brought tighter controls and legal liability for influencers who advertise. Plenty of POS tools exist; none found acts as a compliance and safety co-pilot.

### Cited Findings
- Outlet counts:
  - 61,900 retail outlets: 19,100 pharmacies (nhà thuốc), 39,000 drug counters (quầy thuốc) and 3,800 drug cabinets (tủ thuốc). About 41,000 (66%) were connected to the national drug database (BACKGROUND, November 2019). Source: [VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/hon-66-co-so-ban-le-thuoc-trong-ca-nuoc-da-ket-noi-voi-co-so-du-lieu-duoc-quoc-gia-i38449.html)
  - More recent coverage cites about 60,000 pharmacies. Source: [VietData](https://www.vietdata.vn/vi/post/quy-m%C3%B4-c%E1%BB%A7a-chu%E1%BB%97i-b%C3%A1n-l%E1%BA%BB-d%C6%B0%E1%BB%A3c-ph%E1%BA%A9m-t%E1%BA%A1i-vi%E1%BB%87t-nam) (search summary)
- Chains: Long Châu had 2,025 stores (end of April 2025), Pharmacity more than 1,000, An Khang 326 (end of March 2025). Source: [Doctor247](https://web.doctor247.vn/nha-thuoc-viet-nam-tinh-hinh-thi-truong-thang-5-2025) (search summary; the page itself was unreachable)
- HCMC has 15,611 pharmaceutical businesses and pharmacies. Source: [Tuổi Trẻ](https://tuoitre.vn/he-thong-y-te-tp-hcm-hoat-dong-ra-sao-tu-ngay-1-7-20250630184253815.htm)
- Circular 26/2025/TT-BYT (issued 30/06/2025, effective 01/07/2025):
  - E-prescribing became mandatory for hospitals before 01/10/2025 and for all other facilities before 01/01/2026.
  - E-prescriptions go to the National Prescription System (Hệ thống đơn thuốc quốc gia), linked to the drug database and retail pharmacies.
  - Patient ID (personal ID number, CCCD or passport) is mandatory; children under 72 months need age in months and weight.

## Q7. Regulatory constraints: health data under the PDPL, medical-device software, telemedicine (plus the 2025 AI Law)

### Takeaway
Health data is "sensitive" under the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, effective 01/01/2026). Its 5-year exemption for small enterprises and startups does not cover processors of sensitive data, so any health agent needs explicit consent, a data-protection impact assessment within 60 days and local hosting by design. The AI Law (effective 01/03/2026) requires human oversight and that users know they are dealing with AI. The current high-risk list (from 15/08/2026) covers surgical robotics, not documentation or coordination agents. Avoiding diagnosis claims keeps a product clear of the ambiguous medical-device software rules.

### Cited Findings
- Personal Data Protection Law No. 91/2025/QH15 was passed 26/06/2025 and took effect 01/01/2026; its guiding Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP was issued 31/12/2025. Health data counts as sensitive personal data, with stricter controls and a requirement to tell people their data are sensitive. Sources: [ThuVienPhapLuat (Law)](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Bo-may-hanh-chinh/Luat-Bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan-2025-so-91-2025-QH15-625628.aspx); [ThuVienPhapLuat (Decree 356)](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Quyen-dan-su/Nghi-dinh-356-2025-ND-CP-huong-dan-Luat-Bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan-687428.aspx); [Wincolaw](https://wincolaw.com.vn/vi/nghi-dinh-356-2025-nd-cp-ngay-31-12-2025-huong-dan-luat-bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan.html)
- PDPL obligations and penalties:
  - Consent must be voluntary, explicit and obtained before collection; "silence is not consent".
  - A data-protection impact assessment must be filed within 60 days of starting processing, with a separate assessment for cross-border transfers.
  - Fines: up to 10× illicit revenue for data trading, up to 5% of prior-year revenue for illegal cross-border transfer, and up to 3 billion VND for other violations.
  - Small enterprises and startups may skip the assessment and data-protection officer until 2031, **except** data-service providers, processors of sensitive data, and anyone handling data on 100,000+ people.

#### Agentic workflow (multi-agent, human-in-the-loop)
1. **Intake agent.** Ingests screening results, discharge summaries and patient lists (CSV, EMR export, or a photo of a result sheet via OCR). Stratifies risk using MoH hypertension and diabetes protocols and proposes a care plan; a nurse approves it.
2. **Outreach agent.** Schedules and places Vietnamese voice calls (including Southern accents) or Zalo OA/ZNS messages. It discloses that it is AI (AI Law) and obtains and logs consent (PDPL). It collects readings (or a photo of the BP monitor), symptoms and adherence.
3. **Triage agent.** Applies fixed red-flag rules (for example very high blood pressure, chest pain, stroke signs) and uses an LLM to summarise. Urgent cases trigger an immediate call or SMS to a nurse or doctor and advice to seek emergency care. It never changes medication.
4. **Scheduling agent.** Books confirmatory and follow-up visits through the clinic's calendar or booking API, sends reminders and reschedules no-shows.
5. **Refill agent.** Tracks 90-day prescriptions and reminds the patient, and notifies a partner pharmacy, before they run out.
6. **Memory & reporting agent.** Keeps a longitudinal timeline for each patient and a weekly dashboard (control rate, % reached, escalations). Exports documentation for BHYT family-medicine package claims.

Tools and APIs: telephony with cloud speech recognition and text-to-speech supporting Vietnamese, Zalo OA/ZNS, clinic booking API or Google Calendar, an LLM with function calling, a rules engine, OCR, a dashboard, and optional FHIR export to the EMR.

- W4: nurse dashboard with transcripts, summaries and flags.
- W5: refill reminders and weekly report.
- W6: safety red-teaming (escalation recall on 100 simulated scenarios) and a PDPL impact-assessment template.
- W7: pilot with 30–50 consenting patients or volunteers, or a simulated group drawn from a screening event.
- KPIs: reach rate, readings captured, escalation sensitivity, nurse minutes saved per patient.

- Private polyclinics (351).
- Pharmacy-chain screening events.
- A volunteer group of team members' parents and grandparents (with consent) for usability testing.
- Aim to have an MOU or letter of intent in hand by Round 2.

#### Revenue model & pricing benchmarks
- Charge per enrolled patient per month (assumption: 15,000–30,000 VND) or per completed outreach.
- Clinic SaaS tier with seats for nurses; enterprise contracts with pharmacy chains; later, a share of the BHYT package.
- Benchmarks: Hippocratic AI at about $9 per agent-hour (US); Medpro telehealth consultations at 150,000–1,100,000 VND.

#### Key risks
- Elderly people may not accept AI voices. Mitigation: Zalo, a family-caregiver mode, and a human callback option.

- HCMC private facilities: about 9,886 specialty clinics, 351 polyclinics, 90 private hospitals. Source: [Tuổi Trẻ](https://tuoitre.vn/he-thong-y-te-tp-hcm-hoat-dong-ra-sao-tu-ngay-1-7-20250630184253815.htm)
- Competitors:
  - FPT.eHospital speech-to-text. Source: [FPT IS](https://fpt-is.com/ehospital-2-0/)
  - Glass Health (Vietnamese; $0–200/month). Source: [Glass](https://glass.health/resources/best-multilingual-ai-medical-scribe)
  - Heidi free tier. Source: [Heidi](https://www.heidihealth.com/pricing)
  - Abridge $199–600 per clinician per month and DAX about $600 (estimates). Source: [UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/abridge)
- Global outcome evidence: 16 minutes a day saved (JAMA 2026). Source: [VJST](https://vjst.vn/nhung-cong-cu-ai-ho-tro-bac-si-co-thuc-su-giup-tiet-kiem-thoi-gian-102829.html)

### Inferences
#### Target users & payer
- **Users:** doctors at private specialty clinics, polyclinics and private hospitals.
- **Payers:** clinic owners; later, hospital-system vendors (OEM/white-label) or hospital groups.

#### Market size (assumptions)
- SAM for HCMC: about 10,300 private facilities (sourced) × an assumed 1.5 doctor-seats ≈ 15,000 seats × 790,000 VND per seat per month × 12 ≈ **142 billion VND/year**.
- SOM by year 3: 1.5% of seats (about 225) ≈ **2.1 billion VND/year**.
- A nationwide TAM cannot be computed because no national clinic count was found.

#### Competitors & gap
- Local hospital-system tools offer dictation inside their own software.
- Global scribes list Vietnamese, but none produces Vietnamese EMR forms, Circular-26-compliant e-prescriptions or BHYT fields, and they price in USD.

#### Agentic workflow
1. **Capture agent.** Records the consultation with consent, separates speakers, and transcribes Vietnamese with medical vocabulary and English/Latin drug names.
2. **Note agent.** Fills SOAP or EMR-form fields according to the facility's template.
3. **Coding agent.** Suggests ICD-10 codes and checks that diagnosis and ordered services are consistent, targeting the causes of the Hà Tĩnh and Bà Rịa rejections.
4. **Prescription agent.** Drafts the e-prescription with ID fields, durations, child weight and an interaction check.
5. **Filing agent.** Pushes to the EMR via FHIR/HL7 or the clinic software's API and generates BHYT or private-insurance documents.
6. **Patient agent.** Sends a plain-language Zalo summary and the follow-up date, handing off to Candidate (b).

The doctor reviews, edits and signs every output.

#### MVP scope (about 7 weeks)
- Pick one specialty (for example family medicine, paediatrics or dermatology).
- Record 20–30 role-played consultations (with medical students or volunteer doctors) for evaluation.
- Pipeline: speech recognition → LLM → structured JSON → mock EMR plus e-prescription validator → review screen.
- Metrics: error rate on medical terms, % of fields filled automatically, doctor editing time, rule violations caught.

#### Pilot access near HCMC
- 1–2 private clinics in HCMC. Start with the doctor's dictated summary rather than full ambient recording to reduce consent friction.
- Clinics in alumni and lecturers' networks.

#### Revenue model & pricing benchmarks
- Per-doctor SaaS (assumption: 500,000–1.5 million VND/month), compared with $199–600 for global tools.
- Clinic bundles; OEM licences to hospital-system vendors.

#### Key risks
- Speech-recognition accuracy on Vietnamese clinical speech and regional accents (no benchmark found).
- Hallucinated note content.
- Patient trust in being recorded.
- Lock-in by hospital-system vendors that control integration.
- Low willingness to pay.
- Global entrants (Glass, Heidi) and hospital-system vendors adding ambient features.
- Drifting into diagnosis would bring medical-device software rules into play.

#### Scores (1–5)
1. **Agentic AI depth: 4.** A multi-step pipeline with tool calls (EMR, e-prescription, insurance documents, Zalo), but bounded by doctor sign-off on each visit.
2. **Pain severity & why-now: 5.** EMR deadline 31/12/2026, paper ban 01/01/2027, 7-minute visits, automatic BHYT rejections.
3. **Novelty in Vietnam: 4.** No Vietnamese-native ambient scribe was found; dictation exists inside hospital systems, and global tools list Vietnamese.
4. **Market size & willingness to pay: 3.** About 10,000 private facilities in HCMC, but local software price points are low.
5. **MVP + pilot feasibility: 3.** Technically the hardest (clinical speech recognition, integrations), and recording real visits needs consent and ethics approval.
6. **Regulatory/data/trust risk (5 = low): 3.** Sensitive audio, manageable if it stays documentation-only, hosted in Vietnam and signed by the doctor.
7. **Student booth appeal: 3.** Impressive live demo, but the users are doctors; students relate through illegible prescriptions and long waits.
8. **Judges & mentors: 4.** Strong technical depth and global proof (Abridge, DAX); mentors will probe willingness to pay and distribution.

**Total: 29/40.**

### Gaps
- Benchmarks for Vietnamese clinical speech recognition.
- A survey of clinics' willingness to pay.
- Whether hospital-system vendors (FPT, Viettel, VNPT) expose APIs to third parties.

## Candidate (d): Aftercare-safety & compliance agent for licensed dermatology/aesthetic clinics, RANK 4

### Takeaway
This repositions the saturated "spa CRM/chatbot" idea as medical-grade aftercare. It runs structured check-ins on days 1, 3, 7 and 14 with photos, triages red flags and escalates to a doctor, and documents incidents. It also checks ads and licence scope against the 2025 Advertising Law. Student appeal is strong and the safety pain is real (25,000–35,000 complications a year), but adjacent tools are crowded, small-business prices are low and liability is high.

### Cited Findings
- Complications and enforcement. Sources: [Tuổi Trẻ](https://tuoitre.vn/khoahocphothong/chuan-hoa-linh-vuc-phau-thuat-tao-hinh-tham-my-de-dep-loan-tham-my-chui-104266177.htm); [Tuổi Trẻ KHPT](https://tuoitre.vn/khoahocphothong/tai-bien-sau-phau-thuat-tham-my-co-xu-huong-gia-tang-263318.html)
- Regulation:
  - Spa vs clinic scope under Decree 96/2023. Source: [Tiền Phong](https://tienphong.vn/ranh-gioi-giua-spa-phong-kham-chuyen-khoa-va-benh-vien-tham-my-post1842588.tpo)
  - Influencer liability under Advertising Law 75/2025 from 01/01/2026. Source: [Thanh Niên](https://thanhnien.vn/kol-koc-phai-xac-minh-thong-tin-neu-ro-rang-quang-cao-185251112111634136.htm)
  - HCMC DoH AI monitoring of ad violations. Source: [Medinet](https://medinet.gov.vn/tin-tuc-su-kien/nam-2026-chuyen-doi-so-nganh-y-te-de-benh-vien-bot-giay-dan-bot-cho-doi-so-y-te-c1780-74744.aspx)
- Market:
  - Beauty and personal care $2.74B (2025). Source: [Tiền Phong](https://tienphong.vn/ranh-gioi-giua-spa-phong-kham-chuyen-khoa-va-benh-vien-tham-my-post1842588.tpo)
  - Spa about $661M (2023), rising to $968M (2029), vendor estimate. Source: [Kovitech](https://kovitech.vn/tong-quan-spa-viet-nam-giai-doan-2025-2030.html)
- Competitors and prices:
  - PosApp 220,000–299,000 VND/month (3,500+ spas). Source: [PosApp](https://posapp.vn/phan-mem-quan-ly-spa-tmv)

  - BizCRM and CNV. Sources: [Bizfly](https://bizfly.vn/giai-phap/crm-nganh-spa.html); [CNV](https://cnv.vn/phan-mem-quan-ly-spa-tham-my-vien/) (search summary)

### Inferences
#### Target users & payer
- **Payers:** licensed dermatology and aesthetic clinics and aesthetic hospitals (doctors and customer-care staff).
- **End users:** their clients, mostly young adults.
- Explicitly **not** unlicensed spas that perform invasive procedures.

#### Market size (assumptions)
- No count of licensed aesthetic clinics was found. Assuming about 1,000 in HCMC × 2 million VND/month × 12 ≈ **24 billion VND/year** SAM.
- SOM: 50 clinics ≈ **1.2 billion VND/year**.

#### Competitors & gap
- CRMs send generic aftercare messages and take bookings.
- None found does structured complication monitoring with doctor escalation, documents incidents, or checks compliance.

#### Agentic workflow
1. **Pre-procedure agent.** Contraindication questionnaire, consent and baseline photos.
2. **Aftercare agent.** Scheduled Zalo check-ins capture photos and symptoms. A vision LLM plus rules flags danger signs (for example suspected vascular occlusion or infection), and the doctor is paged within minutes.
3. **Rebooking/retention agent.**
4. **Compliance agent.** Scans clinic posts and influencer scripts for claims that violate the Advertising Law or fall outside the licence scope, and checks practitioner licences through the public lookup.
5. **Incident agent.** Documents a timeline for medico-legal records.

A doctor confirms all clinical triage.

#### MVP scope (about 7 weeks)
- Zalo OA bot, photo intake, rule-based red-flag questionnaire, doctor dashboard and an ad-copy checker.
- Pilot with 1–2 licensed clinics, starting with low-risk procedures (peels, laser).

#### Pilot access near HCMC
- Licensed dermatology and aesthetic clinics in HCMC (count unknown).

#### Revenue model & pricing benchmarks
- 1.5–3 million VND per clinic per month (assumption), compared with about 300,000 VND for PosApp; needs proof that safety and compliance justify the premium.
- Upsell to chains.

#### Key risks
- Liability for missed complications.
- Facial photos are sensitive and biometric data.
- Reputational association with a scandal-prone sector.
- Crowded CRM market; low willingness to pay.

#### Scores (1–5)
1. **Agentic AI depth: 4.** Scheduled multi-step follow-ups, vision triage, escalation and compliance scanning.
2. **Pain severity & why-now: 4.** 25,000–35,000 complications a year; Advertising Law from 01/01/2026; HCMC enforcement.
3. **Novelty: 3.** Spa CRM and aftercare messaging are crowded (PosApp, MONA, BizCRM, CNV); the clinical-safety angle is new.
4. **Market size & willingness to pay: 3.** Big consumer market, but software price points are low and clinic counts are unknown.
5. **MVP + pilot feasibility: 4.** The Zalo plus LLM stack is simple, and clinics in HCMC are reachable.
6. **Regulatory/data/trust risk (5 = low): 2.** Photo-based triage is clinical judgement, the images are biometric, and the sector is sensitive.
7. **Student booth appeal: 4.** Beauty and "is my filler safe?" resonate strongly with students.
8. **Judges & mentors: 3.** Mentors will see a crowded market and liability.

**Total: 27/40.**

### Gaps
- Counts of licensed clinics, clinic software budgets, and evidence that photo-based monitoring detects complications.
