## Q1. EU CBAM definitive period (from 1 Jan 2026): rules after the 2025 Omnibus, Vietnamese exposure, reporting and verification burden

### Takeaway
CBAM has carried real costs since 1 Jan 2026. The Omnibus Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 then added a 50-tonne per-importer annual de-minimis, delayed certificate purchases to Feb 2027 and set the first annual declaration for 30 Sep 2027. As a result, **during 2027 EU importers must obtain verified installation-level emissions data from Vietnamese producers, or fall back on "deliberately punitive" default values**. Vietnam's exposure is concentrated in steel: about 23% of Vietnam's steel exports went to the EU in 2025, worth US$1.94 bn. Cement, aluminium and fertiliser exposure is much smaller.

### Cited Findings
**Rules and timeline**
- The European Commission says CBAM "applies in its definitive regime from 1 January 2026". Importers face "authorisation requirements, reporting obligations and the purchase and surrender of CBAM certificates". The covered sectors are cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. The certificate price is based on EU ETS auction prices. — [European Commission – CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)
- The CBAM Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2025/2083, was adopted on 8 Oct 2025 and entered into force on 20 Oct 2025. Its main changes, per [EY tax alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/eu-adopts-cbam-omnibus-regulation):
  - a 50-tonne per-importer annual threshold;
  - imports may continue while authorisation is pending, if the application for authorised-declarant status was filed by 31 Mar 2026;
  - certificates can be bought and surrendered "only from February 2027, covering emissions embedded in 2026 imports", although costs must still be recognised in 2026;
  - the annual declaration is due by 30 Sep of the following year, starting with reporting year 2026;
  - the €150 consignment threshold is removed;
  - default values may be used more broadly;
  - carbon prices paid in the country of origin can be deducted if verified.
  - Date conflict: CBAMGuide gives the effective date as 17 Oct 2025 ([CBAMGuide](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/de-minimis/)).
- How the threshold works, per [CBAMGuide](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/de-minimis/):
  - It is counted per importer across all CBAM goods combined, per calendar year.
  - It covers iron and steel, aluminium, cement and fertiliser CN codes. Electricity and hydrogen are always in scope.
  - Crossing 50 t mid-year activates obligations retroactively from 1 January, and the importer must be authorised before crossing.
  - Penalties for unauthorised imports are €300–500/tCO₂e.
  - European Aluminium criticises the mass threshold. Its example: aluminium doors for a 200-room hotel weigh about 9.2 t and so escape CBAM.
- The Commission estimate that the threshold exempts about 90% of importers while keeping about 99% of embedded emissions in scope is **[snippet]**, from summaries of [CBAMGuide](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/de-minimis/) and [IntegrityNext](https://www.integritynext.com/resources/blog/article/mastering-cbam-compliance-in-2026-latest-updates-and-how-companies-should-prepare). It was not found on fetch.
- Obligations on non-EU producers, per [Spectreco](https://www.spectreco.com/blog/eu-cbam-2026-simplification-de-minimis-definitive-period):
  - Exporters must supply "verified actual embedded-emissions data at installation level", covering direct and precursor emissions.
  - Actual values require verification by an accredited third-party verifier.
  - Default values are set "conservatively, with a mark-up, so they are deliberately punitive".
  - Recommended steps: map the installation boundary, quantify direct and precursor emissions, build an auditable data trail, and share verified data with the EU importer before the September filing.
- Phase-out of free allocation (share still free): 97.5% in 2026, 95% in 2027, 90% in 2028, 77.5% in 2029, reaching 0% by 2034 — [Vietnambiz, 24 Dec 2025](https://vietnambiz.vn/chuyen-gia-xuat-khau-thep-viet-nam-tiep-tuc-suy-giam-trong-nam-2026-2025122317429524.htm). The same 97.5% (2026) → 0% (2034) path appears in [Tạp chí Kinh tế – Tài chính research, 29 Jun 2026](https://nghiencuu.tapchikinhtetaichinh.vn/danh-gia-chi-phi-carbon-va-rui-ro-tai-chinh-cua-doanh-nghiep-xuat-khau-thep-viet-nam-duoi-tac-dong-cua-co-che-dieu-chinh-bien-gioi-carbon-160153.html).
- Prices:
  - An "official price (Q1 2026)" of €75.36/tCO₂ is reported by [Vietbao, 26 Apr 2026](https://vietbao.vn/thue-carbon-eu-2026-ap-luc-moi-co-the-lam-co-hep-15-thi-phan-thep-nhom-viet-nam-589971.html). I could not confirm it on an EU page.
  - A simulation shows the carbon price rising from about €75 to €147 by 2030 — [Reccessary, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.reccessary.com/en/news/vietnam-esg-forum-taiwan-low-carbon-transition).

**Vietnamese exposure**
- [Vietbao, 26 Apr 2026](https://vietbao.vn/thue-carbon-eu-2026-ap-luc-moi-co-the-lam-co-hep-15-thi-phan-thep-nhom-viet-nam-589971.html):

  - Vietnamese steel averages 2.51 tCO₂ per tonne.
  - Vietnam risks losing about 15% of its market share, and export prices could rise 20–30%.
- Phạm Công Thảo, Deputy General Director of VNSteel and Vice Chairman of VSA, quoted in [Vietnambiz, 24 Dec 2025](https://vietnambiz.vn/chuyen-gia-xuat-khau-thep-viet-nam-tiep-tuc-suy-giam-trong-nam-2026-2025122317429524.htm):
  - Steel exports fell about 25% in 2025, and exports to the EU fell about 38% year on year.
  - The EU's share of Vietnam's steel exports fell from about 26% to about 23%.
  - Under EU default values, the estimated carbon cost is **US$70–80 per tonne**.
  - Exports are expected to keep declining in 2026, especially flat products (HRC, CRC, galvanised).
- Research by Đào Thanh Bình et al. (Hanoi University of Science and Technology), 29 Jun 2026, in [Tạp chí Kinh tế – Tài chính](https://nghiencuu.tapchikinhtetaichinh.vn/danh-gia-chi-phi-carbon-va-rui-ro-tai-chinh-cua-doanh-nghiep-xuat-khau-thep-viet-nam-duoi-tac-dong-cua-co-che-dieu-chinh-bien-gioi-carbon-160153.html):
  - The EU is Vietnam's second-largest steel market after ASEAN, at over 20% of export volume and value.
  - Emissions intensity: BF-BOF 2.35 tCO₂e/t versus EAF 0.62 tCO₂e/t.
  - For 1 Mt of capacity, BF-BOF CBAM cost is about US$84 m in 2026, rising to about US$207 m in 2034. EAF stays at or below about US$55 m.
- [Ximang.vn, 3 Jul 2025](https://ximang.vn/rao-can-cbam-khien-doanh-nghiep-vat-lieu-xay-dung-mat-dan-loi-the-xuat-khau):
  - Steel is 96% of the CBAM-exposed export value among vulnerable product groups.
  - The EU took about 30% of Vietnam's flat-steel exports in 2023–24.
  - Vietnam has 57 cement plants.
- Six product groups are directly hit and about 11 product groups or sub-groups are indirectly affected — [VnEconomy, 26 Nov 2025](https://vneconomy.vn/kiem-ke-khi-nha-kinh-2026-bien-thach-thuc-cbam-thanh-loi-the-cho-doanh-nghiep-xuat-khau.htm).
- From [Reccessary, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.reccessary.com/en/news/vietnam-esg-forum-taiwan-low-carbon-transition):
  - Firms relying on default values rather than verified data "may face significantly higher costs" (Jason Huang, CEO, RECCESSARY).
  - Even non-listed firms "will face increasing demands from clients for verifiable data or risk being excluded from supply chains" (Rebecca Shen, Infinite Dimensions Consulting).

### Inferences
- **The timing fits the competition.**
  - Round 2 runs from 23 Nov 2026 to 10 Jan 2027, and the final is on 27 Feb 2027. These sit right around the opening of CBAM certificate sales in Feb 2027.
  - Both come before the first declaration on 30 Sep 2027.
  - So "2027 is the year EU importers chase Vietnamese suppliers for verified 2026 data" is a defensible why-now story.
- **Cost figures need care.** The 2026 costs of US$70–84 per tonne (VSA; Tạp chí KT-TC study) are far above a simple 2.5% charge.
  - A simple 2.5% charge would be 2.5% × 2.51 t × €75 ≈ €4.7/t.
  - The cited figures only make sense if the free-allocation adjustment uses EU benchmarks (so emissions above the benchmark are charged from day one) and/or default values with mark-ups.
  - Recommendation: present them as industry or academic estimates, not settled 2026 cash costs.
- **The SME market under CBAM is narrower than headlines suggest.**
  - The 50 t de-minimis removes most small EU buyers, and with them the pull on small Vietnamese suppliers who sell to them.
  - The real SME target is producers selling to EU importers above 50 t: distributors, construction contractors and OEMs.
  - Steel and aluminium downstream makers (structures, wire, profiles, fasteners if in scope) are the most relevant SME group.
- **The work is a good fit for agents.** It is multi-step and involves several parties: installation boundary, activity data from invoices and meters, precursor data requested from upstream mills, allocation to CN codes, an evidence pack for the verifier, and answers to importer questionnaires.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the default-value mark-up percentages for 2026–2028, or the published default values themselves.
- I could not retrieve the Commission's December 2025 "scope expansion" package for downstream steel and aluminium goods. The page exists under the title "EU CBAM: Definitive Period Package and Scope Expansion Proposal" at [HKTDC](https://research.hktdc.com/en/article/MjM0OTMyMzA0Nw), but its content was not accessible, so the product list and dates are unverified.
- **I could not confirm whether CN 7318 (screws, bolts, nuts), 7326, 7308 and 7610 are in CBAM Annex I.** The EUR-Lex fetch failed. CBAMGuide lists steel 7301–7307 and aluminium 7601, 7604–7608, 7616. Check Annex I before pitching to fastener SMEs.
- I found no count of Vietnamese installations exporting CBAM goods to the EU, and no market price for CBAM verification of one installation.
- The 2026 quarterly CBAM certificate price is unconfirmed on an official EU source.

## Q2. Domestic GHG obligations, carbon-market pilot, consulting costs and brand (buyer) requirements

### Takeaway
Vietnam's domestic GHG law applies now to about 2,166 large facilities, with an expanded list of about 2,705 expected from 2026. Only 110 thermal-power, steel and cement plants have quotas in the 2025–26 pilot, and the domestic carbon exchange has operated at HNX since 29 Jun 2026. **SMEs therefore feel carbon pressure mainly through global brands (Nike, adidas, H&M, Samsung) and CBAM, not through domestic law.** Prices for GHG consulting are opaque, and FPT itself says its platform is pricey for SMEs.

### Cited Findings
- Decree 119/2025/NĐ-CP was issued on 9 Jun 2025, amends Decree 06/2022, and took effect on 1 Aug 2025. It focuses on four areas: facility GHG inventory reports; quota allocation, carbon-credit trading and the national registry; reduction plans; and reduction reports. It also adds appraisal criteria for inventory results.
  - Sources: [Thư viện Pháp luật](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Tai-nguyen-Moi-truong/Nghi-dinh-119-2025-ND-CP-sua-doi-Nghi-dinh-06-2022-ND-CP-giam-nhe-phat-thai-khi-nha-kinh-625021.aspx) **[snippet]** (the page returned 403 on fetch); [LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/da-co-nghi-dinh-119-2025-nd-cp-sua-doi-nghi-dinh-06-2022-ve-giam-phat-thai-khi-nha-kinh-186-102465-article.html); [Klinova](https://klinova.vn/vi/kien-thuc-xanh/nhung-thay-doi-dang-chu-y-tu-nghi-dinh-1192025ndcp-phan-1.html).
- Phases and limits under Decree 119, per [Klinova](https://klinova.vn/vi/kien-thuc-xanh/nhung-thay-doi-dang-chu-y-tu-nghi-dinh-1192025ndcp-phan-1.html):
  - pilot 2025–2026, phase 2 in 2027–2028, phase 3 in 2029–2030;
  - the carbon-credit offset limit rises "from 10% to maximum 30%" of allocated quota;
  - quota borrowing is capped at 15%;
  - there are national registry obligations.

  - Pilot allocation can start before the Prime Minister approves the total quota.
  - Facilities without quotas must still prepare facility-level reduction plans.
  - Any shortfall is deducted from the next period's quota, and administrative penalties apply under environmental law.

  - The breakdown is confirmed on fetch in [Tạp chí Kinh tế – Tài chính](https://tapchikinhtetaichinh.vn/2705-co-so-se-phai-kiem-ke-khi-nha-kinh-tu-nam-2026-159166.html).

- The 2026 update: **2,705 facilities**, 539 more than the 2024 list. The list is updated every two years and underpins quota allocation and the carbon market for 2026–2030 — [Tạp chí Kinh tế – Tài chính, 18 Jun 2026](https://tapchikinhtetaichinh.vn/2705-co-so-se-phai-kiem-ke-khi-nha-kinh-tu-nam-2026-159166.html). A headline describes the increase as "dự kiến" (expected) — [Baomoi/VnEconomy](https://baomoi.com/du-kien-tang-them-539-co-so-doanh-nghiep-phai-kiem-ke-khi-nha-kinh-c55408920.epi).

### Inferences
- **Who is served today.** Legally mandated demand sits with about 2.2k–2.7k large emitters (around 3,000 tCO₂e/yr and above). Consultancies, FPT VertZéro, Nuoa and EcoCheck already serve them.
- **Where SME demand comes from.** For SMEs the pull is buyers and CBAM. An SME product must be cheap and must produce buyer-specific outputs (brand questionnaires, Higg-style energy data, CBAM templates) rather than only the Decree 06 form.
- **Longer-term upside.** Quota phases in 2027–28 and 2029–30, plus the list rising from 2,166 to 2,705, suggest domestic regulated demand will keep widening.
- **Price ceiling.** If SME GHG service packages really start around 5–11 M VND (unverified), an SME agent subscription probably needs to sit around 10–30 M VND a year, or be sponsored by brands or banks. **(assumption)**

### Gaps
- I could not verify from the decree text the exact facility-report deadlines after Decree 119: annual or every two years, and whether the deadline is 31 March. EcoCheck's "31 Mar 2027" is a vendor claim.
- It is unclear whether the 2,705-facility list has been formally issued to replace Decision 13/2024.
- Decision 263 (pilot quotas) and Decree 29/2026 details were not confirmed on fetch.
- I found no verified price ranges for GHG consulting or verification. Not researched because the search budget ran out: Higg FEM verification costs, and Apple's and Samsung's clean-energy requirements for Vietnamese suppliers.

## Q3. US tariffs 2025–2026, rules of origin and traceability, HS/customs pains, FTA utilisation and self-certification

### Takeaway
US tariffs on Vietnam have swung repeatedly. **Firm-level paperwork cannot avoid the country-wide 12.5% tariff, but it does decide three things:** whether a product qualifies for an exclusion, whether goods count as Vietnamese or as transshipped goods, and how much FTA duty is saved in the EU, UK and CPTPP markets. Meanwhile CBP is tightening origin and transshipment enforcement (EAPA, False Claims Act, isotopic testing), and Vietnam claims FTA preferences on only about 37% of export value. **Origin qualification and origin dossiers are high-value work that is still poorly automated.**

US tariff timeline:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Apr 2025 | 46% tariff threatened |
| Jul–Aug 2025 | 20% reciprocal rate plus 40% transshipment penalty (under IEEPA) |
| 20 Feb 2026 | Supreme Court strikes down IEEPA tariffs |
| 24 Feb – 24 Jul 2026 | 10% Section 122 tariff |
| 24 Jul 2026 onward | **12.5% Section 301 "forced-labour" tariff** |
| due 29 Nov 2026 | Separate Vietnam IP Section 301 determination |

### Cited Findings
**US tariff timeline**
- On 2 Jul 2025 the two sides agreed a framework that "set a 20% reciprocal rate, added a 40% penalty on transshipped goods, and dropped Vietnam's own tariffs on US imports to zero". The April 2025 threat had been 46% — [TradeWithViet](https://tradewithviet.com/blog/vietnam-us-tariff-2026/); [CNBC, 2 Jul 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/02/trump-trade-vietnam-deal.html).
- Executive Order 14326 (July 2025) set the 40% transshipment penalty under HTS 9903.02.01 — [Cosmosourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/vietnam-origin-compliance-transshipment-rules).
- On 20 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA "does not expressly authorise the president to impose tariffs" — [Vietnam Briefing, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/us-supreme-court-blocks-trumps-tariffs-implications-for-vietnam-us-trade.html/). The 6–3 vote is from [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260220-supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-what-now) **[snippet]**.
- Section 122:
  - A 10% tariff on all countries took effect on 24 Feb 2026 for 150 days and expired on 24 Jul 2026 — [Tariffstool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-122-tariff-rates-2026) **[snippet]**.
  - The Court of International Trade struck down the Section 122 tariffs on 7 May 2026. The Federal Circuit stayed that ruling on 11 Jun 2026 — [TradeWithViet](https://tradewithviet.com/blog/vietnam-us-tariff-2026/).
- After 24 Feb 2026 the 40% transshipment penalty "operates under Section 122", with "no authority to mitigate or remit" — [Cosmosourcing, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/vietnam-origin-compliance-transshipment-rules). Its status after Section 122 expired was not found.
- Section 301 investigations ([US-ASEAN Business Council, 6 Apr 2026](https://www.usasean.org/article/section-301-returns-tariff-tool-after-ieepa-ruling-implications-vietnam)):
  - 11 Mar 2026: structural excess capacity, covering 16 economies including Vietnam.
  - 12 Mar 2026: failure to ban forced-labour imports, covering 60 economies including Vietnam.
  - Vietnam's 2025 bilateral surplus with the US was **US$178 bn**, and its global surplus about US$196 bn.
- USTR's June 2026 determination ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/ustr-announces-major-section-301-developments-involving-brazil-vietnam-and-forced-labor-enforcement)):
  - 54 economies "failed to impose and effectively enforce a forced labor import prohibition", and 6 failed to enforce an existing one.
  - A 10% tier applies to Canada, Ecuador, the EU, Indonesia, Mexico, Pakistan, the UK, Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, El Salvador, Guatemala, Malaysia and Taiwan. **All other investigated economies pay 12.5%.**
- Vietnam's position ([Vietnam Briefing, updated 26 Jul 2026](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/us-section-301-forced-labor-investigation-new-trade-compliance-risks-for-vietnam-exporters.html/)):
  - **12.5% applies to most Vietnamese imports from 24 Jul 2026**, with product exclusions in Annex I and II.
  - A textile tariff-rate-quota mechanism covers Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia. **Vietnam is excluded.**
  - Exporters should review "applicable HTSUS classification for each product", eligibility for exemptions, and "supply chain traceability, supplier due diligence, and trade compliance processes".
  - The tariff stacks on top of MFN duties and AD/CVD — [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/07-24-26-client-advisory-new-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-now-in-effect/) **[snippet]**.
- Vietnam IP investigation:
  - Vietnam was named a Priority Foreign Country in the Special 301 Report on 30 Apr 2026. USTR opened a Section 301 investigation on 29 May 2026. Comments were due by 2 Jul 2026, and **a determination is due by 29 Nov 2026** — [USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/may/ustr-announces-section-301-investigation-vietnams-acts-policies-and-practices-related-intellectual); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/ustr-vietnam-section-301-investigation.html); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/ustr-announces-major-section-301-developments-involving-brazil-vietnam-and-forced-labor-enforcement).
  - TradeWithViet reports "a 10 to 12.5% Section 301 tariff" as the working proposal — [TradeWithViet](https://tradewithviet.com/blog/vietnam-us-tariff-2026/).

**Origin enforcement and the documents buyers demand** ([Cosmosourcing, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/vietnam-origin-compliance-transshipment-rules))
- To be "Made in Vietnam", goods need substantial transformation into "a new name, character, or use" (19 CFR 134.1(b)). "Assembly or relabeling of Chinese components does not qualify."
- Documents required:
  - a bill of materials by country;
  - a narrative of the manufacturing process;
  - supplier affidavits;
  - the invoice and packing list;
  - a certificate of origin;
  - production records with lot numbers and dates.
- "Documentation has to exist before the shipment sails."
- How CBP enforces:
  - It uses supply-chain mapping, analysis of HS code patterns, chemical and isotopic testing, and factory visits.
  - EAPA investigations "accelerated through 2025", and DOJ is using the False Claims Act against importers whose origin documents do not hold up.
  - Targeted sectors: furniture (especially wooden bedroom furniture), electronics, textiles and apparel, footwear, steel and aluminium, and solar.

**FTA utilisation, C/O volumes and self-certification**
- MOIT figures ([Công Thương, 3 Sep 2026](https://congthuong.vn/cong-thuong-viet-nam-trong-buc-tranh-toan-cau-dau-nam-2026-bai-4-muoi-bay-canh-cua-471369.html)):
  - Overall FTA utilisation was **37.35% of export value** (2024 data).
  - By agreement: AIFTA 65.12%, ACFTA 41.84%, ATIGA 40.11%, UKVFTA about 36%, EVFTA about 35.1%.
  - Vietnam has 17 FTAs in force, and 78.7% of exports go to just six markets.
  - **Over 1.6 million C/O document sets were received from January to August 2026.**
  - Proposed targets are 55% utilisation by 2030 and 70% by 2035.
  - Causes of low use: SME limits in "compliance, documentation, and supply chain traceability", complex rules of origin, and weak supporting industries.
- [SGGP, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.sggp.org.vn/doanh-nghiep-viet-nam-moi-tan-dung-30-40-uu-dai-fta-post814623.html):
  - Utilisation is 30–40%, with **RCEP at 1.8% and CPTPP at 8.8%**. Vietnam has signed 18 FTAs, 17 in force.
  - Other causes cited: low localisation, and weak financial transparency and digitisation among SMEs.
  - ITPC Deputy Director Hồ Thị Quyên said HCMC "will support businesses through workshops and market information".
- Conflicting figures: another summary cites MOIT (Dec 2025) at about 32–33% overall, EVFTA about 35%, UKVFTA about 38% and CPTPP about 16% **[snippet; source not pinned]**.
- Decentralisation of C/O issuance:
  - Decree 146/2025/NĐ-CP (12 Jun 2025, effective 1 Jul 2025) moved C/O issuance and self-certification approvals to provincial Departments of Industry and Trade — [MOIT, 12 Dec 2025](https://moit.gov.vn/tin-tuc/thi-truong-nuoc-ngoai/bo-cong-thuong-dong-hanh-cung-dia-phuong-thao-go-vuong-mac-ve-xuat-xu-hang-hoa.html).
  - The decree is valid until **1 Mar 2027** unless extended — [Chính phủ](https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?pageid=27160&docid=213914) **[snippet]**.
  - MOIT trainings covered rules of origin, regional value content (RVC) calculation and HS code conversion. Problems cited: limited staff capacity, technical infrastructure gaps, inconsistent procedures, and origin verification.
- Circular 40/2025/TT-BCT (22 Jun 2025, effective 1 Jul 2025) sets conditions for C/O-issuing bodies, including the eCoSys system at ecosys.gov.vn, trained signatories and records storage. It also covers written approval for traders to self-certify origin — [Thư viện XNK](https://thuvienxuatnhapkhau.com/thong-tu-40-2025-tt-bct-nam-2025-quy-dinh-cap-giay-chung-nhan-xuat-xu-va-chap-thuan-tu-chung-nhan-xuat-xu.html).
- A new decree on origin, replacing Decree 31/2018, reached its second draft in May 2025. It covers procedures for self-certification codes, fully online C/O, decentralisation, and a clearer list of origin-fraud acts — [Thư viện Pháp luật](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/chinh-sach-phap-luat-moi/vn/ho-tro-phap-luat/chinh-sach-moi/85691/da-co-du-thao-nghi-dinh-ve-xuat-xu-hang-hoa-thay-the-nghi-dinh-31-2018-nd-cp); [PwC Vietnam, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.pwc.com/vn/vn/publications/news-brief/250613-new-rules-on-goods-origin.html).

**HS classification and customs tooling**
- Thái Sơn's ECUS / ECUS5-VNACCS claims "100,000+ enterprises" as users, 90% of them FDI firms. It has launched **"ECUS.AI (Beta)"** to standardise data and cut manual errors — [Thái Sơn](https://www.thaison.vn/).
- Digicust (EU) claims declaration time falls from 3–4 hours to 10–15 minutes, with 70–90% automation, and that it calculates preferential origin and issues EUR.1 — [Digicust](https://digicust.com/en).

### Inferences
- **How to frame the value.** The firm-level value proposition is "origin integrity plus FTA savings", not "avoid the US tariff". Paperwork does not remove the country-wide 12.5%. It protects against transshipment findings, penalties and False Claims Act exposure, and it decides exclusion eligibility through correct HTS codes.
- **Volume supports per-dossier pricing.** 1.6 million C/O sets in eight months is roughly **2.4 million a year (extrapolation)**. Each set is a multi-document dossier.
- **Provincial departments are a possible channel.** Decentralising C/O issuance to the HCMC, Đồng Nai and Tây Ninh departments creates local partners. It also creates the inconsistency pain the agent can address.
- **Live news during Round 2.** The Vietnam IP determination is due 29 Nov 2026, two weeks after Round 1 closes. If Decree 146 is not extended, its decentralisation provisions lapse on 1 Mar 2027.
- **Underused agreements.** CPTPP and RCEP utilisation is very low (8.8% and 1.8% per SGGP). Rule complexity, such as yarn-forward, cumulation and RVC, is a pain an agent can calculate around.

### Gaps
- Status of the 40% transshipment penalty after 24 Jul 2026, and status of the US–Vietnam reciprocal trade agreement in 2026.
- Not researched: UFLPA detentions of Vietnamese goods, the EU Forced Labour Regulation timeline and CSDDD.
- No Vietnam-specific data on HS misclassification penalties, disputes or post-clearance audits.
- **I did not check whether Vietnamese vendors already sell rules-of-origin or FTA-qualification software beyond ECUS and eCoSys.** Verify before claiming novelty.
- Record-keeping periods and fines for origin fraud under current rules, and whether the new origin decree has been adopted.

### Inferences

#### Idea B (ranked #1): "OriginAgent", an agentic rules-of-origin and export-compliance officer for SME exporters
- **Target users and payer**
  - Users: import-export staff and owners at SME exporters in HCMC (including former Bình Dương), Đồng Nai and Tây Ninh (including former Long An). Typical sectors: garment and footwear subcontractors, wooden furniture, mechanical parts, electronics assembly.
  - Channel: customs brokers and forwarders who prepare C/O dossiers for many SMEs.
  - Payers: the exporter, per dossier or by subscription; brokers, through white-label seats; later, EU or US importers paying for a supplier portal.
- **Pain:** see Cited Findings. In short:
  - utilisation of only about 35–37% on EVFTA and UKVFTA-type agreements, and single digits on CPTPP and RCEP;
  - about 2.4 million C/O sets a year (extrapolated) prepared largely by hand;
  - US buyers now require BOM-by-country files, process narratives and supplier affidavits *before sailing*;
  - False Claims Act and EAPA exposure for weak documentation.
- **Why now (dated):**
  - 24 Jul 2026: 12.5% Section 301 tariff on Vietnam.
  - 29 Nov 2026: IP Section 301 determination due, during Round 2.
  - 1 Jul 2025: C/O issuance decentralised to provinces. If not extended, Decree 146 lapses on 1 Mar 2027.
  - New origin decree in drafting, with self-certification codes and a clearer list of fraud acts.
  - Proposed target of 55% FTA utilisation by 2030 and 70% by 2035 ([Công Thương](https://congthuong.vn/cong-thuong-viet-nam-trong-buc-tranh-toan-cau-dau-nam-2026-bai-4-muoi-bay-canh-cua-471369.html)).
- **Market size (assumption)**
  - TAM (dossiers): about 2.4 M C/O sets a year × 40–80k VND per automated dossier ≈ **96–192 bn VND/yr**.
  - TAM (subscriptions): about 10,000 exporting SMEs × 12–24 M VND/yr for origin, tariff monitoring and US evidence packs ≈ **120–240 bn VND/yr**.
  - SAM: assume the Southern belt is about 40% of this. This is **unverified**; the region's export share was not found.
  - SOM after 36 months: about 100 SMEs × 18 M VND + 5 brokers × 120 M VND ≈ **2.4 bn VND ARR**.
  - None of the unit prices are sourced. Validate them in the Round-1 survey.
- **Competitors and gap**

  1. *Intake agent* pulls the purchase order, BOM (Excel or ERP), purchase invoices, import declarations for inputs (ECUS export files) and supplier declarations from email, Zalo or Drive.

**Shortlist: (1) B, OriginAgent; (2) A, CarbonClerk, ideally narrowed to CBAM supplier data for downstream metal SMEs plus a brand-questionnaire autopilot; (3) C, ThợCả AI, the safest build and best booth demo but weak novelty; (4) D, EUDR traceability, only if other domain reports do not already cover it.** Pitch B within the Variant E "Export Compliance Passport" story.
